**Prepared for:** Tzvi & the Vectis founding partners
**Scope:** 1:1 AI upskilling competitors, cohort/exec-ed context, adjacent high-ticket coaching analogs, B2B referral partner map, and strategic recommendations for the Vectis Residency line.

## Executive Summary

The premium 1:1 AI upskilling market — defined here as $300/mo+ recurring, individual-focused, human-led — is **structurally underdeveloped relative to demand**. Three things are true at once:

1. **The cohort/exec-ed end is crowded and cheap-to-mid-priced.** Maven AI tracks ($1–$10K), Section AI ($349/yr individual; ProfAI), Anthropic's free AI Fluency course, Google's Professional Certificate, and Wharton/MIT/Oxford ($2.5–15K) all teach the same headline — "use AI strategically" — at scale. None of them are a 1:1 mentor.
2. **The 1:1 end is fragmented and brand-thin.** The closest direct analogs (Concierge Studio, Michael Rolph, AI Coworker Blueprint, Practica) are mostly solo operators with project-based packaging or sub-$300 chatbot products. There is no recognizable "Reboot of AI coaching" yet.
3. **The classic executive coaching world (Reboot, Torch, BetterUp, Pivot Method) shows the price corridor.** Premium 1:1 retainers settle in the **$1,000–$3,000/mo** band; mid-tier sits at $500–$1,000/mo; on-demand B2C platform coaching anchors at $71–$223/mo.

This means Vectis's chosen $500–$1,000/mo target slot is **the right zone — it's the empty middle between BetterUp-style B2C ($71–$223) and Reboot-style premium ($2K+)**, and it's exactly where the operator ICP Tzvi described will pay without escalation. The differentiating wedge is *content category* (AI fluency, not leadership), *positioning* ("secret superpower"), and *delivery quality* (warm dark + brass editorial brand vs. generic SaaS).

The biggest strategic asymmetry uncovered in this scan is **Section D**: the B2B referral partner map. Several professional services categories — fractional CFOs, boutique exec coaches, bookkeepers, M&A advisors, fractional CMOs/CTOs — are *actively and structurally* unable to serve their clients' AI questions. They are sitting on warm, qualified, recurring referral flow with nothing premium to send people to. Vectis can become the default AI partner for several of these categories within 12 months.

### Deep teardowns of the four most relevant direct competitors

#### 1. Concierge Studio — the closest direct analog
*Hero:* **"I build your *personal AI* so you don't have to."**
*Sub:* "A white-glove service that architects a custom AI system around your workflows, your business, and your life."

This is the cleanest reference for what Vectis is trying to do. Rodrigo Conde sells three tiers — $5K Essentials, $7.5K Professional, $15K Sovereign (local-LLM setup) — to founders, execs, and investors. The build session is 4–6 hours; clients receive documented assistants, automations, dashboards. Voice is technical-confident-warm. Design is ~8/10 (clean, dark, slightly portfolio-y).

**What's working:** Productized white-glove. "I do the hard part for you." Clear ICP ("running companies, not debugging prompts"). Concrete proof claim ("12+ hours per week saved").
**What's weak:** Project-based, not retainer-led. The retainer attaches to the build, not the relationship. Brand reads "premium freelancer" rather than "firm." No discreet/secret framing — explicitly testimonial-driven.
**Founder visibility:** High — Rodrigo's name and face anchor the entire site.

**For Vectis:** This is the strongest pricing reference for *project-led* AI advisory. If you sell a one-off "build my system" SKU at $5–7K, this is the comp. But Vectis Residency's monthly retainer thesis lives upstream of this, in the *sustained relationship* lane Rodrigo doesn't fully claim.

#### 2. Michael Rolph — AI Fluency Coaching
*Hero:* **"Build the AI fluency required to work intelligently and creatively in a world reshaped by automation and intelligent systems."**

A British executive coach who repositioned to AI fluency. Sells against Microsoft Work Trend Index data ("75% of knowledge workers use AI") and explicitly addresses the three buyer questions: *"Will AI replace me?" / "Do I need to be technical?" / "Is there real ROI?"* Offers a complimentary intro session, structured 8-week-style outcomes ("-40% time on briefs"), and lists a "starter kit" of 5 domain prompts as the on-ramp.

**What's working:** Tight ICP language. Ties AI fluency to existing exec-coaching delivery model — buyers already understand the format. Clear answer to "Why not just take a course?" embedded in copy.
**What's weak:** Generic exec-coach design (6/10). No "secret AI superpower" hook. Pricing not transparent — feels gated.
**Founder visibility:** Personal-brand-first, single coach.

**For Vectis:** This is the closest verbal positioning. The vocabulary — "AI fluency," "force multiplier," "data hygiene/output review/policy-aligned usage" — is sophisticated and worth borrowing selectively. But Rolph has no firm/multi-partner story; Vectis can outclass him on brand alone.

#### 3. AI Coworker Blueprint — Liz Baker
*Hero:* **"AI for us, not to us."**
*Sub:* "Operational infrastructure that compounds with every interaction."

Liz Baker (20+ years at Verizon, TIAA, MeadWestvaco) sells a *productized* coaching deliverable: a single strategic document — the "AI Coworker Blueprint™" — that the buyer pastes into ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini. Then 23+ role-specific templates. Then "build a team of AI coworker-coaches." Quote framework on site is excellent — *"People complain that AI is an echo chamber that just tells you what you want to hear. This setup ensures that never happens."*

**What's working:** Brilliant product packaging — converts "coaching" into a paste-able artifact, defensible and ownable. Strong corporate-credentialed founder. Persona-led template SKUs (CEO, consultant, freelancer, nonprofit director +23). Heavy testimonial use from Fortune 500 names.
**What's weak:** Conversion path is unclear — site implies workshops/templates more than ongoing 1:1. No retainer surfaced.
**Founder visibility:** Liz's bio anchors the brand; she's both the face and the methodology owner.

**For Vectis:** The Blueprint pattern — "one strategic document you paste into your AI, that compounds every interaction" — is a *deliverable* idea Vectis Residency should steal directly. Make a "Vectis Operator Brief" that lives at the heart of every Residency client's stack.

#### 4. Practica — the failed-marketplace tell
Originally a $200/hr human exec-coaching marketplace; pivoted to a $10–$20/mo AI coaching app (with the human option still available at ~$3,000/yr). Co-founder Whittemore on TechCrunch: *"The average per-hour price was $200…the average person who does a full year of coaching spends about $3,000 a year."*

**For Vectis:** This is the most important data point in Section A. Practica had the network and the marketplace and *still couldn't sustain $3K/yr per individual buyer at scale*, so they abdicated to AI at $10–20/mo. **The reason matters: their buyer was self-paying mid-career professionals.** Vectis's $150K–$350K operator ICP has 3–5x the discretionary spend, has a key inflection 2–5 years out, and is buying *strategic insurance*, not skills development. That's the buyer Practica didn't reach.

### What's missing in the direct-competitor field

- **No firm-with-partners brand.** Every premium operator is a solo. A 4-partner firm with editorial design is a defensible visual posture.
- **No "discreet/secret" positioning anywhere.** Everyone's voice is loud, productivity-coded, LinkedIn-thirsty. Vectis's "no one has to know about us" is genuinely unowned.
- **No cohesive Israeli/UK premium brand.** The Israeli landscape is enterprise consulting (Bell, HI4.AI, Intersog) or academic (TAU/Nebius); no premium 1:1 individual coaching brand.

### The white space cohorts leave for high-ticket 1:1

1. **No personalization to the buyer's actual P&L or book of business.** Every cohort teaches the median student.
2. **No discretion.** Cohort logos and LinkedIn announcements are the price of admission.
3. **No persistence.** A cohort ends; the buyer's AI evolution doesn't.
4. **No accountability for application.** Maven Reflections, Section's ProfAI — these are nudges, not partners.
5. **Identity mismatch.** A senior IC at a law firm or a $5M founder-operator isn't going to sit in a Zoom cohort with 80 strangers and admit she doesn't know what an MCP is.

This is where Vectis lives.

### Voice pattern from premium retainer coaches

Things they do that Vectis should adopt:
- *Single-page coaching mechanics.* "How it works" is its own URL with FAQ-grade detail (Pivot Method's `/coaching/monthly` page is a textbook example).
- *Confidentiality stated explicitly.* "Our sessions are 100% confidential."
- *Cancel anytime, no min/max.* Removes purchase friction at the consideration stage.
- *Confidentiality + no-rescheduling-beyond-month + 24-hour cancel.* Tight terms that signal seriousness.
- *Testimonial framing as inner-circle:* Pivot calls it the "Pivot Coaching inner circle."

Things they do that Vectis should *avoid*:
- "I will go to the mat for you" / generic uplift language.
- "Your dream life awaits."
- Stock photos of women laughing on couches.
- Overuse of "transformation," "unlock," "potential."

## Section D — B2B Referral Partner Map (HIGHEST-VALUE NEW WORK)

This is where Vectis can compound fastest. Each partner category below has three traits in common: (a) their clients ask AI questions they cannot answer, (b) they have no good vendor to send those clients to, and (c) the relationship economics already exist (formal fees, reciprocity, or relationship-based).

### Referral economics decoder

Across this space three patterns repeat:
- **Formal referral fees** — common in finance-adjacent (CFOs, wealth, M&A) at **10–20% of first-year revenue** or **a flat finder's fee ($1K–$10K)**. Often disclosed-in-engagement-letter with the end client.
- **Reciprocity / relationship-based** — dominant in coaches, fractional execs, agencies. *No money changes hands*; deals flow because both parties make each other look good and add value to a shared client.
- **Equity for intros / advisor shares** — used by VC scouts, angel groups; less relevant for Vectis's buyer.

The right move for Vectis: **default to reciprocity-first, optional 10% structured fee on disclosed referrals**, with a private "Vectis Partner Network" page that gives partners a co-brandable PDF and a calendar link.

### The 10 partner categories ranked

#### 1. Fractional CFO networks 🟢🟢🟢 (highest leverage)
**Why they refer:** Their clients (the SMB CEO, the $5–50M founder-operator) come to them because something is broken financially. Within 3 sessions, the CFO realizes operations/data/AI is the next bottleneck. CFOs hate scope creep into IT.
**Economics:** Most fractional CFO firms (Paro, FocusCFO, B2B CFO, Preferred CFO, Kruze, Ascent, Astero, CFO Advisors) are **structurally referral-driven** — they pay 10% on AUM-sticky deals and accept relationship-based on others. The bigger networks (Paro, Zeni) won't formalize, but **independent fractional CFO partners absolutely will.**
**First contact:** Don't pitch Paro. Pitch the **30-CFO independent network at firms like Preferred CFO, FLG Partners, or boutique CPA-shops with fractional CFO arms**. Open with: *"We're the AI partner you don't have. Bring us into your next client where the founder asked about AI."*
**Bring to relationship:** A 2-page "AI Diagnostic for Your Client's First Quarter" PDF the CFO can hand the founder; a co-branded Loom intro; lifetime 10% of first-year retainer.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets warm pre-qualified intros to $1–10M revenue founder-operators (Vectis SMB ICP) AND personal referrals into the founder for Residency. Gives the CFO a missing service line and a cleaner client relationship.
**Status note:** Tzvi's existing call with **Tal Moji (bookkeeper) is the proof of pattern.** The same pattern exists at every fractional CFO. Pursue this category first.

#### 2. Boutique executive coaches 🟢🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** Their clients keep raising "I can't keep up with AI" in coaching sessions. The coach is psychologically equipped, not technically. They lose credibility when they hand-wave it.
**Economics:** Pure relationship-based. Coaches have explicit codes against fee-splitting in many ICF lineages. **Don't offer money — offer the coach a free Residency seat for themselves.**
**First contact:** Independent coaches in Reboot's orbit (Reboot only has ~30 named coaches; many ex-Reboot coaches operate solo), Pivot Coaches network, female founder coaches, ex-McKinsey leadership coaches on LinkedIn. Open with: *"Your client just asked you about AI again. We're who you send them to. No fee. We'll send back."*
**Bring to relationship:** A standing 30-min "AI consult" you'll do for any of their clients free. Co-branded resource list. Reciprocal referral when one of your Residency members needs leadership/identity work.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets the warmest referral in the world — coach-to-client. Gives the coach AI legitimacy and a no-conflict outlet.

#### 3. Bookkeepers / accountants / boutique CPA firms 🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** Same logic as #1 but earlier in the value chain. The bookkeeper sees the founder weekly. They're being asked to "automate the books with AI" and don't have the partner.
**Economics:** Relationship- and fee-based; CPAs are comfortable disclosing 10–15% referral arrangements via engagement letter.
**First contact:** Tal Moji is the template. Build out 5–10 IL-based bookkeepers and 5–10 US-based boutique CPA firms ($1–10M revenue clientele). Avoid the Big 4 (they're competition); target firms of 5–25 people.
**Bring to relationship:** A "We make your client look like a tech-forward shop" pitch. A diagnostic call you'll do free. Branded one-pager for them to email to clients.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets steady SMB pipeline; gives the accountant a genuine differentiator vs. the boring CPA next door.

#### 4. Business brokers / lower-mid-market M&A advisors 🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** Post-acquisition is when AI gets installed. PE acquirers and search funders explicitly ask "how do we modernize this with AI?" the week of close. Vectis Residency is also a fit for the *individual* search funder pre-acquisition (he's the operator buyer).
**Economics:** Brokers and PE advisors love formal referral economics. **10–15% of first-year fees, paid in writing**, is standard.
**First contact:** Search-fund networks (Stanford/HBS search-funder lists), Axial-listed M&A advisors in the $5–50M range, ETA Twitter/LinkedIn community. Israeli search-fund equivalents exist but are smaller.
**Bring to relationship:** A "Day 1 AI Playbook for Newly-Acquired SMBs" deliverable. Pre-acquisition: a Vectis Residency seat for the searcher.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets the highest-LTV SMB engagements in the market (PE/searcher deals are 5–7-figure); gives the broker/advisor a value-add that closes deals.

#### 5. Boutique brand & web agencies that DON'T do AI 🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** Their clients ask about AI weekly. They've decided not to build the capability (smart — it's a different muscle). They need a partner so they don't lose the relationship.
**Economics:** Relationship-based + revenue share on referred work (15–20% standard among agency partnerships).
**First contact:** Israeli design studios with strong international books (Pentagram-style boutiques, smaller editorial-design firms). US: Read Forefathers, Ueno-alumni shops, brand strategy firms like Manual or Studio Output. Open with: *"Your clients are asking you about AI. We'll be your invisible partner. Co-brand or white-label, your call."*
**Bring to relationship:** White-label option from day one. Co-branded deck. A bone-clean intake brief that doesn't make their client feel handed-off.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets enterprise-flavored SMB deals through an existing trusted relationship; gives the agency a credible AI answer.

#### 6. Industry-specific consultants (healthcare ops, legal ops, real estate) 🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** Vertical operators are buying *outcomes* (more billable hours, fewer denials, faster closes), and AI is now the lever. The consultant doesn't want to learn it themselves.
**Economics:** Mostly relationship-based. Some legal-ops firms will do disclosed 10% structures.
**First contact:** Healthcare RCM consultants, legal ops consultancies (read CLOC member firms), real estate brokerage tech advisors. Israeli legal-tech and Israeli health-tech vertical advisory firms are strong targets given the partner's geography.
**Bring to relationship:** Vertical-specific case studies (build them — even one apiece). A "how AI shows up in [your vertical] this quarter" briefing you'll do for their roster.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets hyper-qualified vertical deals; gives the consultant a productized AI add-on without staffing it.

#### 7. Wealth managers / private bankers 🟡 (high asymmetry, slower)
**Why they refer:** Their HNW clients (founders post-exit, partners at firms, family offices) are asking about AI as both an investment thesis AND a personal tool. Wealth advisors are completely unequipped to coach them on the personal-tool side. AI fee-compression is also a real industry pressure (per Cerulli/AdvisorHub data).
**Economics:** Strict relationship-based + reciprocity. **No fee splits — wealth advisors are fiduciaries.** The currency is "you make my clients love me more."
**First contact:** RIAs in the $1–5B AUM range, multi-family-office heads, private bankers at boutique IL/UK shops. Cold outreach is dead here — needs warm intro.
**Bring to relationship:** A standing "AI for your top 20 clients" salon you'll co-host once a quarter. Branded client-facing PDF: *"AI for the Family Office Principal."*
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets the highest-quality individual buyer in the entire market (post-exit founders, principals, partners). Gives the advisor a genuinely differentiated white-glove offering.
**Caveat:** Long sales cycle. Plant seeds; harvest in 12+ months.

#### 8. VC scouts / angel groups / accelerators 🟡
**Why they refer:** Their founders need AI literacy to compete. Some funds will pay for it as a portfolio benefit.
**Economics:** Mixed — some funds pay vendor invoices on portfolio behalf, some refer for reciprocity, some want advisor-share.
**First contact:** Israeli funds (Aleph, TLV, Vintage, Disruptive), seed funds with operator-led portfolios, accelerators (Techstars, 8200, MassChallenge IL). **The Agentic Israel community Sam co-founds is the obvious wedge here.**
**Bring to relationship:** "AI for Founders" portfolio talk you'll give for free. Discounted Residency cohort for portfolio founders.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets founder pipeline; gives the fund a portfolio-services line item.

#### 9. Fractional CMOs / CTOs 🟢🟢
**Why they refer:** When their scope expands beyond marketing/eng (CMO needs AI marketing ops; CTO needs AI for the non-eng side of the business), they hand off. They are *constantly* in scope-expansion conversations.
**Economics:** Strong reciprocity — fractional execs all refer to each other. Some have formal partner programs. (Fractional CMO networks like Chief Outsiders, Marketers Without Borders run formal partner motions.)
**First contact:** Chief Outsiders, Marketers Without Borders, FractionalGTM, and the major fractional-CTO networks (Bolster, NetCTO, Fractional). Israeli fractional ecosystem is smaller — direct approach to individual fractionals on LinkedIn.
**Bring to relationship:** A clean delineation of who owns what. (Vectis owns AI fluency + AI implementation; CMO/CTO owns their function.) Reciprocal referrals.
**What Vectis gets vs. gives:** Gets steady mid-market deal flow; gives the fractional an AI partner that doesn't compete with their core scope.

#### 10. Executive search / recruiting firms 🟡 (slowest, but high-status)
**Why they refer:** AI literacy is now a hire criterion. Search firms are quietly evaluating candidates on it; some clients ask the firm to "find someone who's actually used AI." Senior candidates between roles are Vectis's perfect Residency buyer.
**Economics:** Strict relationship-based; search firms are extremely protective of their candidate relationships and will not take fees that look like kickbacks.

## Section E — Strategic Synthesis & Recommendations

### 1. ICP confirmation

**Tzvi's $150K–$350K operator slice is correct. Tighten it slightly.**

The market scan validates this band as the empty middle:
- Below $150K personal income, buyers convert to $20–$500 AI courses (Eliason, Maven, Section).
- Above $350K, buyers either (a) buy $5–15K productized engagements (Concierge Studio) or (b) get coaching as a corporate benefit (BetterUp/CoachHub via employer).
- The $150–$350K operator self-pays *or* expenses it through their LLC and treats it as *strategic insurance against being the one in the room who can't use AI.* Nobody is serving them well right now.

**Tighten by adding a psychographic filter to the demographic one:** *Operators who already pay $200+/mo for a coach, advisor, executive assistant, or premium gym/wellness service.* Recurring-premium-service buyers are the ones who'll convert. Test this in early intake calls.

**Keep ruling out:** early-career, students, ML engineers, AI-curious-but-unfunded.

### 2. Pricing architecture

**Three-tier monthly retainer, plus a one-time on-ramp:**

| Tier | Price | Format | Ideal buyer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic** (one-time) | **$1,500** | 90-min intake + 2-week build of a "Vectis Operator Brief" + 1 follow-up | Anyone — also serves as discovery for the retainer. |
| **Residency — Standard** | **$650/mo** | 2 × 60-min 1:1 / month + async Slack/email | Senior IC, director, mid-level professional |
| **Residency — Plus** | **$1,200/mo** | 4 × 60-min / month + Slack between sessions + quarterly strategy session | Founder-operator $1–10M, fractional consultant, partner-track professional |
| **Residency — Cabinet** (cap at 5–8 clients) | **$2,500/mo** | Weekly 1:1 + on-demand async + quarterly in-person if relevant | Executives & founders who want a true "AI advisor on retainer" but still want to learn |

This lands you cleanly between Pivot's $997 single-tier reference and Reboot's higher band. The Diagnostic tier becomes the discovery motion that converts to retainer.

**Don't:** Sell hourly ($200–$300/hr signals freelancer, not firm).
**Don't:** Sell single-session packs (BetterUp owns that floor; you'd be racing them down).
**Do:** Make the $650 tier the public anchor. Cabinet is invitation-only.

### 3. Packaging

- **Lead with 1:1.** Cohorts dilute the "no one has to know" promise.
- **Light cohort optionality** as a *Cabinet member salon* — quarterly closed-door dinners (NYC, Tel Aviv, London) where Cabinet members meet each other. Don't sell it as a feature; deliver it as a perk.
- **Session cadence: bi-weekly minimum.** Anything more spread becomes irrelevant given AI's pace.
- **Slack/email between sessions: yes, but bounded.** Pivot Method's wording is good: "email in lieu of the call if you can't make it." Position as elastic-but-present.
- **Standing artifact: the Vectis Operator Brief.** A single living document the client owns, updated monthly, that they paste into ChatGPT/Claude. Steal Liz Baker's pattern; do it better.

### 4. Positioning

**Yes — lead with "secret AI superpower / discreet advisory."** Three reasons the data supports it:

1. **Nobody else owns it.** Every direct competitor is loud. The discreet executive search world (Cowen Steward, Sloane Shorey, JMJ Phillip) shows the lane is rich and unowned in coaching.
2. **The ICP psychology rewards it.** Senior operators don't want to LinkedIn-announce that they hired a coach to teach them what their younger team already knows. Discretion is the product.
3. **Your brand visual system (warm dark + brass + editorial serif) is already discreet-coded.** Loud positioning would fight your design.

**Sub-position:** "An AI cabinet for your career inflection." This grounds the abstract "secret superpower" in the operator's specific moment (partner track, displacement risk, business expansion).

### 5. Voice patterns — strong vs. weak hero candidates

**Adopt these patterns (paraphrased from real strong examples):**

✅ "Build the AI fluency required to lead in a world reshaped by automation." *(Rolph)*
✅ "An AI cabinet for the next two years of your career." *(Vectis-original)*
✅ "Your secret AI advantage. No one has to know."
✅ "We don't teach AI. We calibrate it to you." *(differentiates from cohorts)*
✅ "AI for us, not to us." *(Liz Baker — works because it's directional)*
✅ "Quiet, monthly, and yours." *(discretion + retainer + ownership in 4 words)*
✅ "Decisions, not demos." *(separates Vectis from prompt-engineering content)*

**Avoid these clichés:**

❌ "Unlock your full potential" / "Transform your work" / "10x your productivity" — generic.
❌ "Your AI co-pilot" / "Your personal AI" — overused, blurs human-vs-tool boundary.
❌ "The future of work is here" — empty.
❌ "Master AI in 30 days" — defaults to course-tier voice.
❌ "AI-powered everything" — diluted.
❌ "Demystifying AI" — patronizing to your ICP.
❌ "Stay ahead of the curve" — flattened from overuse.

### 6. Three highest-leverage referral partner categories to pursue first

1. **Fractional CFOs** — first move: convert Tal Moji conversation into a written reciprocal memo template; book 5 IL/US fractional CFO intros in 2 weeks; co-branded "AI Diagnostic" PDF.

### 7. Should the Residency sit equal to the SMB line on the homepage?

**No — quieter secondary section.** Three reasons:

1. **Buyer is different.** SMB implementation buyer is a CEO writing a check from a company; Residency buyer is an individual writing a check from their LLC or personal account. Two homepages-in-one will confuse both.
2. **Voice is different.** Implementation = confident, ROI-coded, technical-spec-led. Residency = discreet, editorial, warm. They share the brass-and-warm-dark visual, but the *register* is different.
3. **Residency is a referral-and-community-driven product, not an SEO product.** It doesn't need homepage real estate the way the SMB line does — it needs a clean linkable URL.

**Recommended layout:**
- **Hero / primary section:** SMB implementation. Loud about value and outcomes. ROI proof. Logos.
- **Secondary section, lower on homepage:** A muted, editorial card titled *"Vectis Residency — A private AI cabinet for senior operators. By introduction or application."* Single sentence. Single CTA: "Request Access." Link to a quieter standalone Residency page that uses more of the editorial-serif/brass treatment than the SMB sections.
- **Standalone Residency URL** (`/residency`) does the heavy positioning work — long-form manifesto, discreet voice, no testimonials with full names (initials + role only), private partner page link.

This setup matches your brand's "mentor-friend-partner" energy: both lines benefit from the firm's authority, but the Residency's discretion is reinforced by *how it's presented*, not just what it says.
