# The AI Implementation Agency Landscape: Research Pass #1 for Vectis

**Prepared for:** Vectis (vectisbuild.co) — 4-partner SMB AI implementation firm based in Israel
**Scope:** ~30 firms across US, UK/EU, Israel, plus premium-boutique reference points
**Bias of the analyst:** Opinionated, design-filtered, SMB-anchored. No punches pulled.

---

## 0. Executive Summary — What the Scan Actually Reveals

Three things jumped out and they should shape every Vectis decision:

1. **The category is overcrowded at the bottom and surprisingly thin at the top.** There are hundreds of "AI automation agencies" with Webflow-template sites, Make.com screenshots, and copy that all sounds the same ("we automate the boring stuff so you can focus on growth"). There are very few firms — fewer than ten globally that we'd call genuinely 9/10+ on the integrated package of positioning + design + voice + offer clarity. That gap is Vectis's whole opportunity.
2. **Almost nobody is bundling AI implementation with brand systems.** The "AI + brand integrated" lane that Vectis is targeting is essentially empty. Design-forward firms (Clay, Lazarev, Nelson Cash) do brand and product UX but don't sell AI implementation as a recurring practice. AI implementation firms (Automatic, AE Studio, Northslope, LeftClick) explicitly *don't* do brand. Vectis's bundle thesis is real white space.
3. **SMB-focused AI implementation pricing has crystallized into a recognizable shape.** Setup fees $10K–$40K, retainers $2.5K–$7.5K/mo, with productized workflows ($1,500–$6,000) at the low end and fractional CAIO retainers ($8K–$25K/mo) at the top. Vectis's planned $5K floor is on the right axis but should be pitched against an explicit ROI/value frame, not hours.

The most important strategic input from this scan: **the bar for "trustworthy AI implementation partner" is set by language and visual register, not by case studies.** Most prospects can't evaluate technical depth. They evaluate the website. Firms that look like they ship (Linear-tier UI craft, technical-spec language, real metrics, a senior face) win over firms with longer client lists but generic decks.

## 1. Category Map — Dominant Positioning Archetypes

The market currently sorts into roughly seven recognizable archetypes. Each one has clichés to avoid and a structural weakness Vectis can exploit.

| Archetype | Representative firms | What they sound like | Structural weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. AI Automation Agency (Make/n8n/Zapier-flavored)** | LeftClick AI, MQLFlow, Ronins, GiantKelp, Beyond Agency | "We automate the boring work so your team can focus on growth." | Indistinguishable from each other; race-to-the-bottom on price; YouTube-creator-led brands. |
| **B. Senior-led AI Implementation Partner** | Automatic (hireautomatic.com), AE Studio, Headstart NYC | "Senior people on the actual problem. We ship in six weeks." | Too founder-dependent; rarely scales past $200K MRR without losing voice. |
| **C. Forward-Deployed Engineering / Vertical AI** | Northslope (Palantir-native), Faye, Elemental Cognition | "We embed engineers. We ship production systems, not slideware." | Aimed at upper mid-market and enterprise; pricing locks out SMB. |
| **D. AI Product / UX Design Studio** | Clay, Lazarev.agency, Cieden, Adam Fard, Parallel, Noomo | "We design AI products that feel like billion-dollar software." | They design AI; they don't *implement* AI inside a client's operations. |
| **E. AI + Brand Integrated** | (Largely empty. VCCP/faith and Superside hint at it from creative-agency side. Tom & Co. dabbles.) | Mostly enterprise creative agencies adding GenAI capability. | No SMB-focused operator owns this. **This is Vectis's lane.** |
| **F. Fractional CAIO / Strategic AI Leadership** | Chief AI Officer, Faye, SYRV, ISHIR, AI-First Mindset, Boardman, 51 Degrees | "Hire a Fractional Chief AI Officer for 40-60% less than a full-time hire." | Strategy-heavy, implementation-light; mostly older consultants in suits, weak design. |
| **G. AI Upskilling / Coaching** | Section (Galloway), Maker School (Saraev), various 1:1 coaches | "Make your team AI-fluent." | Mostly group cohorts; the 1:1 high-ticket lane is wide open. |

**Where Vectis fits:** Sit between B (senior-led implementation) and E (AI + brand integrated), with G (1:1 upskilling) as the second product line. Borrow the operational tempo of C (Forward-Deployed Engineering language) and the visual register of D (design studio craft). That four-way intersection is unoccupied.

**White space identified:**
- AI implementation + brand systems + diagnostics, sold as one bundle to $1M–$50M revenue companies.
- Senior, design-forward, *quietly global* (no city-flag bragging, no "London-based" lede).
- Operational-tempo brand language (T0/T1/T2/T3) that nobody in this market is using yet.
- High-ticket 1:1 AI upskilling at $500–$1000/mo — almost everyone else does cohorts.

## 2. Deep Teardowns — The Standout 10

### 2.1 Automatic — hireautomatic.com
**This is the closest analog to what Vectis is becoming. Study it carefully.**

- **Hero copy (verbatim):** "Your business, on autopilot where it should be." Subhead: "Automatic is a senior-led AI implementation and strategy partner. We sit beside you on the workflows, client relationships, and vendor operations you already run, scope the work to the actual problem, and build AI into it so your team spends less time on the work nobody wanted."
- **Service shape:** Five named practice areas (AI Strategy & Adoption, AI Workflow Automation, Client Experience AI, Operations Intelligence, SEO/AEO/GEO). Engagements are scoped per-problem, not packaged.
- **Pricing (published — rare and valuable):** Setup $10K (single tight engagement) up to $40K+ (cross-functional). Retainers $2.5K–$7.5K/mo. First production system in six weeks or less.
- **ICP:** Two explicit verticals: agencies (30-person) and SMBs (up to 200-person logistics/services). Founder Nick — a previous agency operator.
- **Voice:** Direct, clipped, slightly cocky, operator-to-operator. "No PDF deliverable." "No juniors learning on your retainer." "A workflow sprint for a 30-person agency isn't shaped like a vendor-intelligence rebuild for a 200-person logistics SMB. Pretending they are is how consultancies end up selling wrong-shaped work." This is voice gold.
- **Design (8.5/10):** Clean, restrained, dark-friendly, light dose of Linear influence. Numbered stats hero ("6 wk / 38% / $420K / 92"). Section breaks labeled "01 / 02 / 03." Prose-heavy in the right places. Slightly under-designed on type hierarchy but that's part of the directness. Their "01 · Week 1 / 02 · Week 2" cadence is a near-cousin of Vectis's T0/T1/T2/T3 — Vectis should go further.
- **What's working:** Pricing is on the page. ROI baseline is named. Voice is unmistakable. Process is concrete (Diagnose → Design → Ship → Keep going). FAQ answered "in writing."
- **What's weak:** Founder is named in the newsletter ("Nick"), but the brand otherwise reads slightly faceless. The case studies aren't deep. SF/NY/Remote footer feels generic.
- **Founder visibility:** Medium. Nick is in copy but not over-personalized.
- **Steal:** the published price band, the per-engagement scoping language, the "we don't sell software" framing.

### 2.2 AE Studio — ae.studio
- **Hero:** "AE does frontier alignment research and production AI work under one roof."
- **Service shape:** Product development, data science, machine learning, AI solutions, product design, neurotech, plus AI alignment research as a halo line.
- **Pricing:** Not surfaced. Reference points from market: typically $50K–$500K+ engagements; senior-led pods.
- **ICP:** Top founders, Fortune 10 executives, PE portfolio companies, alignment-aware orgs. Not really SMB — but the *pose* is instructive.
- **Voice:** Confident, intellectual, mission-driven. "Bootstrapped since 2016, no outside investors, 150 senior professionals." "The team top founders and Fortune 10 executives call when AI needs to actually ship."
- **Design (8/10):** Slightly overstuffed nav; uses warmth and editorial typography. Strong "Human Agency" thematic anchor — they sell a *worldview*, not a service.
- **What's working:** A philosophical anchor (human agency, alignment) that gives the brand gravity. Bootstrapped + independent narrative is genuinely differentiating.
- **What's weak:** The site is sprawling — 12+ landing pages with similar headers. Hard to find pricing or the actual entry product.
- **Founder visibility:** Distributed — many named partners and engineers in "Team" page; no single hero face. **This is a useful model for Vectis given Tzvi's constraint.**
- **Steal:** the philosophical anchor; the team-page-as-credibility-instrument pattern.

### 2.3 Northslope — northslope.com
- **Hero:** "Forward-deployed AI applications built on Palantir. Palantir's only Vanguard Elite partner delivering mission-specific AI for aerospace, energy, finance & more."
- **Service shape:** Forward-Deployed Engineering pods that embed inside customer companies. "Working MVPs within days of kickoff." 90%+ of staff are Palantir alumni.
- **Pricing:** Not surfaced. Pre-Series A; Series A $22M (Jan 2026). Mid-six to seven figures per engagement implied.
- **ICP:** Industrial, energy, healthcare, aerospace, finance — mid-market+ companies "where differentiated operations are a strategic necessity."
- **Voice:** Mission-language. "We only take on valuable problems." "We move from idea to impact in days, not months." "Outcomes, not activity." "Forward Deployed Engineering" used as proper-noun branding.
- **Design (8.5/10):** Tight, technical, minimal, slightly aerospace-flavored. Heavy on outcome metrics ("nearly 7x revenue growth"). Dark + restrained.
- **What's working:** The FDE concept gives them a proprietary noun. Provenance (Palantir alumni) is the credential.
- **What's weak:** Far too enterprise-coded for SMB; reading their site, an SMB owner would feel locked out.
- **Founder visibility:** Bill Ward (founder/FDE) is named; humble. Not the brand's center of gravity.
- **Steal:** the proprietary-noun discipline (Vectis's T0/T1/T2/T3 should function similarly), the "outcomes not activity" framing, the alumni-credential play (Faropoint, MTV, US Bank, etc. — once Tzvi can speak).

### 2.4 Headstart NYC — headstart.nyc
- **Hero:** "AI-Native Applied Intelligence." Tagline: "Building retrieval-powered AI agents that combine your internal knowledge with reasoning and action to solve complex, real-world tasks."
- **Service shape:** Five service axes — agentic AI, document intelligence, training/eval frameworks, data integration, applied retrieval.
- **Pricing:** Not surfaced. Reads as $50K–$250K mid-market.
- **ICP:** Mid-market companies with messy internal data; not over-specified.
- **Voice:** Restrained, technical, a touch academic. Almost too restrained — leaves money on the table by not naming the buyer's pain.
- **Design (8.5/10):** Clean editorial layout, generous whitespace, monospaced accents. The **.nyc TLD is a quietly excellent geographic flex** — owns the NYC AI craft signal without needing to scream it. Vectis should consider a similar "quiet but specific" TLD move (vectisbuild.co already does this well).
- **What's working:** "AI-Native Applied Intelligence" is a phrase that reads as proprietary. Strong typography hierarchy. Site loads fast and feels technical-first.
- **What's weak:** No prices, no case studies surfaced on hero, no faces. Trust is implied, not earned.
- **Steal:** the proprietary phrase pattern; the typographic restraint.

### 2.5 Clay (clay.global)
- **Hero:** "We build transformative digital experiences for the world's leading brands by blending AI, design, and technology."
- **Service shape:** UX design + branding + web + AI-powered UX. Three engagement models: T&M, fixed fee, retainer.
- **Pricing:** Blended rate; project-dependent. Range based on Clutch data: $50K–$500K+.
- **ICP:** B2B SaaS, AI-native products, fintech. Clients include Amazon, Snapchat, Slack, Sony.
- **Voice:** Polished, slightly corporate, design-led. "We focus on creating real connections."
- **Design (9/10):** This is a 9 because of typographic discipline, project-page craft, and color restraint. Genuinely beautiful. Bright/light register — the opposite of Vectis's warm-dark, but the *quality bar* is the reference.
- **What's working:** The portfolio does the selling. Case studies are shipped products you can verify.
- **What's weak:** Increasingly faceless. The brand reads like an agency, not a studio with a point of view.
- **Founder visibility:** Low. No founder face on home or about. **Important data point for Vectis: at scale, you can run faceless. Pre-scale, you can't.**
- **Steal:** project-page craft; engagement-model transparency; how to write about hybrid AI+design without overclaiming.

### 2.6 Lazarev.agency
- **Hero:** "Top 14 AI design agencies transforming businesses with smart UX." (Their hero is curatorial — they publish category-defining lists, including themselves on top.)
- **Service shape:** AI UX/UI design, conversational AI design, MVP design, design systems. They don't build the AI, they design the interface to the AI.
- **Pricing (published — exceptional):** "POCs/UX audits/small AI demos: $5K–$15K. Comprehensive UX and agent design: $15K–$35K. Full AI-native products or enterprise systems: $50K+."
- **ICP:** Fintech, SaaS, Web3, e-commerce, real estate — funded startups and scaleups.
- **Voice:** Strong opinions. "Don't treat AI UX as a template. Think of it as the craft of designing how intelligence behaves." "If an agency struggles to answer, they're likely tooling-first, so just skip them."
- **Design (9/10):** Editorial, dark-friendly, motion-rich without being gratuitous. The blog is part of the brand.
- **What's working:** They publish strong opinions, then position themselves inside those opinions. Pricing is transparent. Brand has a real voice.
- **What's weak:** Their core service (AI UX design) is narrower than they pretend. They don't implement.
- **Steal:** the "publish a category map; place yourself inside it" content pattern. Vectis should publish "The four kinds of AI agency you should never hire" within 90 days.

### 2.7 Cieden
- **Hero:** "Top AI Consulting Firms for Small Businesses (2026 Buyer's Guide)." Like Lazarev, they lead with a buyer-guide content piece.
- **Service shape:** Product strategy, AI UX, design systems, MVP design.
- **Pricing:** Not surfaced. Project-based.
- **ICP:** SMB-leaning startups; they explicitly call out the "Fortune 500 budget" trap.
- **Voice:** Helpful, comparative, slightly journalistic. Not overly designed in tone — earns trust by being clearly useful.
- **Design (8/10):** Solid; not exceptional. Their content design is stronger than their hero design.
- **What's working:** Their "honest comparison" content is one of the best buyer-education plays in the category. Worth modeling.
- **What's weak:** Hero positioning is a list, not a promise. Their own brand identity is the weakest part.
- **Steal:** the SEO-via-buyer-guide flywheel.

### 2.8 LeftClick AI — leftclick.ai
- **Hero:** "AI-driven growth systems for agencies. Founded by Nick Saraev — 400K YouTube subs, #1 Skool community, tens of millions generated for clients."
- **Service shape:** Funnel audits → fixed-price builds → optional retainers. Productized.
- **Pricing:** Fixed-scope project model with optional retainer. Audit is free (30 min).
- **ICP:** Agencies and service businesses; secondary mid-market/enterprise via workshops.
- **Voice:** Operator-to-operator, slightly bro-flavored, very results-forward. "Featured in Forbes, WSJ, Fortune, BBC."
- **Design (7/10):** Functional, conversion-optimized, not crafted. Reads like a YouTuber's funnel — because it is.
- **What's working:** **The creator funnel is the entire business.** Saraev's YouTube → Skool → Agency. Vectis won't replicate the YouTube scale, but the **community-first inbound model (Agentic Israel) is structurally similar and undervalued in this category.** The Saraev playbook is a proof point that community + content + productized offer beats cold outbound.
- **What's weak:** Brand is one-person-dependent and visually generic. If Saraev disappears, so does the firm.
- **Founder visibility:** Maximum — entire brand is Nick's face.
- **Steal:** the community-funnel mechanic. **Sam fronting Agentic Israel is the LeftClick playbook for Vectis** — but with a less-bro voice.

### 2.9 Faye / Fayedigital.com (Fractional CAIO arm)
- **Hero:** "AI is moving at lightspeed, and it can't be treated like a standard IT project." Service: Fractional Chief AI Officer.
- **Service shape:** Strategic Roadmap → Data Governance → Cross-Departmental Alignment → Performance Measurement. Mid-market ICP.
- **Pricing:** Not surfaced. Industry signal: $8K–$25K/mo for fractional CAIO retainers.
- **ICP:** Mid-market and enterprise; explicitly "not ready for a permanent CAIO."
- **Voice:** Corporate, careful, governance-flavored. "Strategic 'north star.'"
- **Design (7/10):** Standard mid-market consultancy. Functional, not memorable.
- **What's working:** Clear category ownership ("Fractional CAIO") + bundled services (Faye Agent Portal, AI Workshops). Productization.
- **What's weak:** Voice and design are interchangeable with ten other firms in the same lane.
- **Steal:** the productized service-line architecture (Vectis can have a "T2 Accelerate Sprint" or "AI Diagnostic" as productized SKUs the same way Faye has "AI Workshops").

### 2.10 Section (sectionschool.com — for upskilling reference)
- **Hero (paraphrased from ecosystem):** "AI fluency for the way you actually work." Scott Galloway-backed.
- **Service shape:** Cohort-based AI courses; AI fluency for execs; team training.
- **Pricing:** $400–$2K per cohort; team plans.
- **ICP:** Mid-career professionals and exec teams.
- **Voice:** Smart, casual-academic, Galloway-flavored.
- **Design (8.5/10):** Editorial, warm, modern. Closer to Anthropic-warmth than Linear-cool.
- **What's working:** The "AI fluency" frame is more honest and durable than "learn ChatGPT." Cohort scarcity drives urgency.
- **What's weak:** Cohort model leaves the high-ticket 1:1 lane wide open. **This is exactly Vectis's $500–$1000/mo upskilling product opportunity.**
- **Steal:** the "fluency, not tools" framing for the upskilling line.

## 4. Pricing Reality Check (Specific Dollar Ranges)

Based on triangulation across 25+ firms with surfaceable pricing:

| Engagement Type | Floor | Median | Ceiling | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AI Diagnostic / Readiness Assessment** | Free (loss-leader) | $2.5K–$5K | $15K–$25K | Most agencies use it as a free gateway. Charging for it is differentiating; $5K is a credible anchor. |
| **Single-workflow productized build** | $1.5K–$2.5K | $3K–$6K | $10K–$15K | Very competitive bottom — tools-first agencies undercut hard. |
| **Mid-range AI implementation project** | $10K | $15K–$35K | $50K | This is the most common SMB band. Automatic's $10K–$40K window is the canonical reference. |
| **Premium AI implementation** | $50K | $75K–$150K | $250K+ | Enters the Tier-2 mid-market consultancy band (per Bushe.co's tier framework). |
| **Monthly retainer (basic)** | $500 | $1.5K–$2.5K | $3K | Tools-first agencies; n8n maintenance only. |
| **Monthly retainer (SMB AI partner)** | $2.5K | $4K–$6K | $7.5K | The Automatic / hireautomatic.com band. **Vectis's $5K floor sits clean inside this.** |
| **Monthly retainer (Fractional CAIO)** | $5K | $8K–$15K | $25K+ | Strategic-leadership flavor; less hands-on. |
| **1:1 AI Upskilling (the Vectis $500–$1000 line)** | $200 (cohort) | $400–$1K | $3K+ | Almost no one does true 1:1 in this band. Cohorts ($300–$2K) dominate. **Vectis's pricing here is on the right axis but should be presented as "executive-grade" to anchor the price.** |
| **% of cost saved / value created (model Vectis is considering)** | 10% | 15–25% | 33%+ | Rarely surfaced as a primary pricing model in this segment — most firms layer it on top of fixed retainers. **Recommendation: don't lead with %-of-value pricing on the website.** Use it as a private upgrade path on Tier 1 retainers. It's hard to verify and creates trust friction in the first call.

**Strategic pricing recommendation for Vectis:**

- **$5K floor retainer is correctly priced.** Don't go lower. Lower clients are not the ICP.
- **Project setup fees of $15K–$40K are credible.** Anchor them with a published range on the site, like Automatic does. This is a major trust signal.
- **Charge for the diagnostic.** A paid diagnostic at $3K–$5K filters lookers from buyers and creates a case-study artifact.
- **Hold the % of value model in reserve.** Use it for proven ICPs after one cycle, not on the website. Public value-pricing language creates more skepticism than confidence in SMB.
- **For the $500–$1000/mo upskilling line: position as "1:1 executive AI residency."** Don't try to compete with cohort pricing. Position against fractional coaching ($500–$2K/mo for executive coaching is normal).

## 5. Design Quality Distribution

Across the ~30 firms surveyed, the rough distribution:

- **9–10/10 (genuinely exceptional craft):** ~15%. About 4–5 firms.
- **8/10 (strong, distinctive, well-executed):** ~25%.
- **7/10 (competent but generic):** ~35%.
- **5–6/10 (template-tier, weak):** ~25%.

**The 9+/10 references for Vectis's visual research input:**

1. **Clay (clay.global)** — typographic discipline, color restraint, project-page craft. Bright register, but the *quality bar* is the standard.
2. **Lazarev (lazarev.agency)** — editorial dark-friendly motion, opinionated content design.
3. **Linear (linear.app)** — not in scope as competitor but the visual gold standard for "clean technical" the Vectis brief references.
4. **Anthropic (anthropic.com)** — the warm-intellectual register; serif manifesto + technical chrome. The other Vectis reference.
5. **Koto (koto.com)** — for craft + warmth at brand-agency scale.

## 6. Voice Analysis — What Works, What Fails

### 6.1 Hero copy that works (verbatim)

1. **Automatic:** *"Your business, on autopilot where it should be."* — Possessive ("your"), confident verb ("on autopilot"), boundary ("where it should be"). Promise + restraint in eight words.
2. **Northslope:** *"Forward-deployed AI applications built on Palantir."* — One proprietary noun, one credential. No adjectives. Pure spec language.
3. **AE Studio:** *"AE does frontier alignment research and production AI work under one roof."* — Names two opposite things in one sentence. Generates curiosity.
4. **Lazarev (interior):** *"Don't treat AI UX as a template. Think of it as the craft of designing how intelligence behaves."* — Reframes the buyer's job. This is the most powerful sentence pattern in the category.
5. **Headstart:** *"AI-Native Applied Intelligence."* — Three words, all proprietary-feeling. Reads like a product, not a service.
6. **Faye:** *"AI is moving at lightspeed, and it can't be treated like a standard IT project."* — Names the buyer's exact misframing. Earns the next click.
7. **Automatic (interior):** *"AI isn't the bottleneck. The integration is."* — Two short sentences that completely reposition the category.

### 6.2 Hero copy that fails (composite of common patterns)

1. *"Transform your business with AI."* — Used by ~40% of firms. Means nothing.
2. *"We build AI solutions that drive real business impact."* — "Real," "drive," "impact" are all contentless qualifiers.
3. *"From strategy to implementation, we're your end-to-end AI partner."* — Generic three-letter language.
4. *"Unlock the power of AI for your business."* — "Unlock the power of" is the marker for an undifferentiated firm.
5. *"AI-driven solutions for the modern enterprise."* — Compounding clichés.
6. *"Custom AI solutions tailored to your business."* — Every consultancy on Earth claims this. Says nothing.
7. *"We help you navigate the complexities of AI."* — Hedging, soft, anti-confidence.

### 6.3 Voice patterns that recur in the strongest firms

- **Short declarative sentences. Then a contrast sentence.** ("AI isn't the bottleneck. The integration is.")
- **Operator-to-operator language.** ("No juniors learning on your retainer.")
- **Naming the buyer's failed prior attempt.** ("You've tried ChatGPT. Someone built a Zap. A vendor sold you a 'copilot.'")
- **Boundary statements.** ("We don't sell software." "We only take on valuable problems." "If you need a 50-person rollout, this isn't us.")
- **One proprietary noun used consistently.** (Forward-Deployed Engineer; AI Employee; Formal Knowledge Model; Gemini Enterprise Practice.)
- **Numbers in hero block.** (Automatic's "6 wk / 38% / $420K / 92" is the gold standard.)

**For Vectis:** the brand voice brief (Engineered to compound. Designed to stand out. T0/T1/T2/T3) is pointed at exactly these patterns. Push it harder. The operational-tempo language is a proprietary-noun system; treat it like one.

## 7. Bundling Patterns — The Real White Space

How many firms in the scan bundle AI implementation with brand/design/marketing?

- **Pure AI implementation, no brand:** ~70% (Automatic, Northslope, AE Studio, LeftClick, Powered_by, Markovate, Faye, all the Fractional CAIOs).

This is the single most important strategic finding in this scan. **The Vectis bundle thesis is genuinely defensible because the market hasn't built it yet.**

Caveat: there is a *reason* nobody has built it. AI implementation talent and brand-systems talent rarely coexist in one firm — they're different ICP, different rate cards, different staffing models. Vectis's 4-partner structure (CTO + CMO + COO + CEO) maps cleanly to this multi-disciplinary requirement, which is part of why this is buildable for this team specifically.

## 10. Strategic Recommendations — What Vectis Should Learn from This Scan

This is the synthesis section. Direct, opinionated, actionable.

### 10.1 Positioning

**Pick one sentence and own it.** The strongest hero in the entire scan is Automatic's: *"Your business, on autopilot where it should be."* It's possessive, it has a verb, it has a boundary. It works because it doesn't try to do everything.

Vectis's "Engineered to compound. Designed to stand out." is doing two jobs at once and that's actually fine — *if* it's the brand line, not the hero promise. The hero needs to name the buyer's specific situation. Recommended hero pattern:

> **"Most companies know they should be using AI but don't know where to start. We bundle AI implementation, brand systems, and operational design into one engagement — engineered to compound, designed to stand out."**

That's not the final copy, but the structural pattern (specific buyer state → specific bundle → brand line as closer) is what the strongest firms are doing.

**Lead with the bundle, not the AI.** Saying "AI agency" puts Vectis in a category with 500 firms. Saying "we bundle AI capacity + brand systems + operational design for SMBs ready to compound" puts Vectis in a category of one. Lean into the bundle as the proprietary frame.

**Geo-quiet is correct.** Don't lead with Israel. The .co TLD, the geo-neutral name, and the partner team distributed across timezones is the right move. Reference the work, not the location. You'll rarely lose a US deal because of where you're based; you can lose one because the site felt "foreign" before they read the case study.

### 10.2 Pricing & Packaging

1. **Publish the price band.** $15K–$40K setup; $5K–$10K/mo retainer. Putting numbers on the site is the strongest single trust signal Vectis can make. Almost nobody in the design-forward lane does this. Automatic does. It works.
2. **Build three productized SKUs.** Map them to the operational tempo:
   - **T1 Align — AI Diagnostic** ($5K, 2 weeks). Paid, scoped, becomes a deliverable artifact. Not free.
   - **T2 Accelerate — Implementation Sprint** ($25K–$40K, 6–10 weeks). The flagship build.
   - **T3 Compound — Partnership Retainer** ($5K–$10K/mo). The renewable revenue base.
3. **The 1:1 upskilling line is its own thing.** Position it as **"Vectis Residency"** or similar — 1:1 monthly engagement at $500–$1000/mo with a senior partner. Don't bundle it with the implementation line on the homepage. It's a different ICP (individual operators/execs) and dilutes the implementation pitch.
4. **Hold % of value pricing for renewals.** Don't put it on the marketing site. It becomes a Tier 2 conversation after a successful T2 sprint. Public value-pricing creates more friction than confidence at the SMB tier.

### 10.3 Brand Voice

The Vectis brief — warm dark, brass accent, editorial serif manifesto, technical-spec language, T0/T1/T2/T3 — is sharper than 90% of the category and should not be diluted. Specific recommendations:

1. **Use the operational tempo as a proprietary noun system.** T0 IDLE / T1 ALIGN / T2 ACCELERATE / T3 COMPOUND should appear on the homepage, in proposals, in invoices, in retainer reports. It's the Northslope "Forward-Deployed Engineer" move. Don't be embarrassed to repeat it.
2. **Write short, declarative, contrast sentences.** The Automatic pattern: "AI isn't the bottleneck. The integration is." Vectis can run a similar pattern: "The problem isn't AI. It's that nothing talks to anything else."
3. **Name the buyer's failed prior attempt.** Almost every Vectis prospect has tried ChatGPT, hired a freelancer, or built a Zap. Naming that explicitly on the site earns the next click. (Automatic does this perfectly.)
4. **Do not, under any circumstances, write "transform your business with AI" or "unlock the power of AI."** They appear on ~40% of category sites and are the surest signal of an undifferentiated firm.
5. **Lighten the visual register slightly as the brief instructs.** The warmth needs to come through in interior pages and in the writing — the warm-dark + brass alone reads slightly cold. Editorial body copy with serif breathing room (Anthropic-style) closes the gap.

### 10.4 Founder Visibility & Trust

1. **Distributed partner page from day one.** Sam, Adam, Isaac with photos and operator-credible bios. Tzvi referenced in the founding story without being named or pictured. When the time comes (post-Faropoint), add him as the fourth face. The site doesn't need restructuring.
2. **Sam fronts Agentic Israel; Agentic Israel fronts Vectis indirectly.** Don't make Vectis the explicit sponsor of every event — let the community become the funnel. The LeftClick playbook works precisely because the community comes first.
3. **First three case studies do massive work.** Shua Seif (nursing-home referral intake) is a perfect first case — a specific industry, a specific workflow, a measurable outcome. Build it with a published case study format (Lazarev's pattern) — problem → architecture → outcomes with numbers. Aim for three of these by end of Q1.
4. **A founders' newsletter modeled on Automatic's "One or two dispatches a month from Nick. Reply if you want."** Personal, low-volume, direct. Sign it from one of the four. This builds intimacy without requiring Tzvi's name.

### 10.5 Sales Motion & Funnel

1. **Agentic Israel as primary funnel** is correct. Treat it as an asset, not a side project. Track the conversion path explicitly: community member → workshop attendee → diagnostic call → T1 Align engagement.
2. **LinkedIn engagement second.** The strongest plays in this category right now are content-led LinkedIn presence by named partners. Adam (CMO) should own this from day one. Sam should publish from his community-builder identity. Isaac and Tzvi can be quieter.
3. **Outbound automation last.** Don't lead with cold outreach — the ICP (SMB owners $1M–$50M revenue) is saturated with AI-agency cold pitches. The differentiation has to come from the warm-channel arrival or the prospect won't even open the deck.

### 10.6 What to Build in the First 90 Days (Concrete)

1. A 9/10-design website with **published pricing band**, **named partner team** (3 of 4), and **three productized SKUs** mapped to T1/T2/T3.
2. **One published opinion piece** in the Lazarev pattern — e.g., "The Four Kinds of AI Agency You Should Never Hire" or "Why Most AI Implementations Fail in Month Three" — designed to be shared in Agentic Israel.
3. **A paid $5K AI Diagnostic** as the only entry product on the site. No free discovery. The diagnostic IS the discovery.
4. **The Shua Seif case study** as the first proof point. Then two more by end of Q1.
5. **A monthly founder dispatch** signed by one partner, low-volume, high-personality.
6. **The Vectis Residency** ($500–$1000/mo 1:1 upskilling) as a separate landing page, separate funnel, not on the homepage.

## 11. Closing Read

The strongest finding of this scan is that the SMB AI implementation category is not winning on technology — it's winning on positioning, design craft, and offer transparency. There are perhaps five firms globally that have all three. None of them are bundling AI + brand + ops the way Vectis intends to. None of them are using operational-tempo language as a proprietary noun system. None of them are positioned at the warm-intellectual register the brief describes.

**The competitive risk is not that the lane is full. It's that the lane is empty for a reason — building a four-partner firm that genuinely delivers on a multi-disciplinary bundle is hard.** The pricing has to make sense. The team has to actually have brand + AI + ops competence. The first three engagements have to ship cleanly enough to become marketing.

If Vectis ships a 9/10 site, publishes pricing, names three partners, gets the Shua Seif case study live, and runs Agentic Israel as a real community asset, it will sit in the top 5% of the global SMB AI implementation category on launch day. That's a defensible position to compound from.

The brand brief is sharper than the category. The team composition matches the bundle thesis. The community funnel is structurally undervalued. The only thing left to do is ship the work — and then write about it the way the strongest firms in this scan do: short sentences, real numbers, named operators, and one proprietary noun system repeated until it becomes the way the buyer thinks about the problem.
